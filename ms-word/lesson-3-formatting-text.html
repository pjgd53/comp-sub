🎨 Formatting Text

How to select your words, then make them bold, colorful, or a different style.

🖱️ Selecting Text First

Before you can change how any text looks, you need to select it — this tells Word exactly which letters or words you want to change. Selected text turns a highlighted blue color.

There are a few ways to select text: click and drag across the words with your mouse or finger, double-click a single word to select just that word, or press Ctrl + A to select your whole document at once.

B = Bold, I = Italic, U = Underline — click a highlighted button here to turn that style on for your selected text.

Bold makes text thicker and darker — great for important words. Italic tilts text sideways — often used for titles or emphasis. Underline draws a line beneath the text.

Try It! Select a whole sentence and click Bold. Then click it again — notice the style turns back off! Most formatting buttons work like a light switch: click once to turn on, click again to turn off.

🔤 Font, Size, and Color

Next to Bold/Italic/Underline are two dropdown boxes: one changes the font (the style of the letters, like Calibri or Times New Roman), and the other changes the font size (how big or small the text is — a bigger number means bigger text). There's also a colorful A with a red bar under it for font color, and a highlighter-pen icon for adding a highlight color behind your text, just like using a real highlighter on paper.

✅ What You Learned

  • Select text first (click-drag, double-click, or Ctrl+A) before formatting it.
  • B / I / U buttons apply Bold, Italic, and Underline.
  • Dropdown boxes let you change the font and font size.
  • The Font Color and Highlight tools add color to your text.
  • Most formatting buttons toggle on and off — click once for on, again for off.
