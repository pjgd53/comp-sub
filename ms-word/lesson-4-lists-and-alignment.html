📋 Lists and Alignment

Turning plain sentences into organized bullet points, numbered steps, and neatly lined-up text.

• Bullet Points and Numbered Lists

When you have a list of items — like things to bring on a trip, or steps in a recipe — typing them as a list makes them much easier to read than one long sentence. Word has two list buttons in the Home tab's Paragraph group:

The bullet-list button adds a dot before each line; the numbering button counts 1, 2, 3 instead.

Click the Bullets button to start a bulleted list — every time you press Enter, a new bullet point appears. Click the Numbering button instead, and Word automatically numbers each line 1, 2, 3, and keeps renumbering them correctly even if you add or remove items later!

Try It! Turning a list on or off works like the format buttons from Lesson 3 — click once to start a list, and click the same button again to turn it off.

↔️ Aligning Your Text

Alignment controls where your text sits between the left and right edges of the page. There are four alignment buttons, right next to the list buttons:

LEFT ALIGN (the default)
This paragraph starts at the left edge.
CENTER ALIGN
Great for titles and headings!
RIGHT ALIGN
Good for dates in the corner of a letter.
JUSTIFY
Justify stretches each full line so both the left and right edges line up neatly, like a newspaper column.

Left align is what Word uses automatically — your text hugs the left edge, like normal handwriting. Center align puts each line in the middle, which looks great for a title. Right align pushes text to the right edge. Justify stretches the spacing so that both edges of a paragraph line up evenly, the way text looks in a newspaper or book.

✅ What You Learned

  • The Bullets button adds a dot before each list item.
  • The Numbering button counts your list items 1, 2, 3 automatically.
  • The four alignment buttons are Left, Center, Right, and Justify.
  • Center align is a popular choice for titles and headings.
