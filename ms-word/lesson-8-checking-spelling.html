🔍 Checking Your Spelling

Let Word help you find and fix mistakes — but always read your work too!

〰️ What Do the Colored Lines Mean?

As you type, Word quietly checks your words. When it finds something that might be wrong, it draws a line under it:

Red wavy line

A word that might be spelled wrong, like bananna.

Blue double line

A possible grammar problem, like She go to school.

🖱️ Fixing One Word (Right-Click)

  1. Right-click the word with the red or blue line.
  2. A small menu shows suggestions at the top.
  3. Click the correct one — Word fixes it for you!

If the word is actually right — like a name such as "Sorsogon" — choose Ignore Once to remove the line.

📝 Checking the Whole Document (F7)

Press F7, or go to the Review tab and click Spelling & Grammar (in newer Word this button may be called Editor). Word jumps to each mistake one by one so you can Change it or Ignore it.

Word Is Not Perfect! Spell check only knows if a word is a real word — not if it’s the right word. "I red a book" has no red line, but it should be "read"! Always read your work yourself before saving or printing.
Try It! — Mistake Hunt Type this sentence exactly: "My freind and I goed to the libary yesturday." How many red lines do you see? Fix them all using right-click, then press F7 to make sure nothing is left.

✅ What You Learned

  • A red wavy line = possible spelling mistake. A blue double line = possible grammar mistake.
  • Right-click the word and pick a suggestion to fix it.
  • F7 or Review › Spelling & Grammar checks the whole document.
  • Spell check can miss mistakes — always re-read your work.
